Replies are understood automatically for DNS-over-HTTPS (Answer), Safe Browsing (matches), URLhaus (query_status) and simple APIs returning malicious, blocked or similar. For anything else, set Flag field to the reply field that indicates a threat. A reply that is not understood (for example an invalid-key error) counts as "no answer", never as "safe".

Help

How it works

When you open an email in Gmail (or another supported webmail: Outlook, Proton, Yahoo, AOL, Zoho, iCloud, Fastmail or GMX), the extension looks at every link and works out where it really goes, warns you about dangerous or misleading ones, and quietly removes invisible tracking images. It needs no setup.

1. Email openedLinks and images in the message are read in your browser.
2. Decoded on your deviceSecurity and tracking wrappers (Proofpoint, Microsoft SafeLinks, Barracuda, Google, Facebook, ad and affiliate trackers and 50+ more) are unpacked locally.
3. Short links looked upIf the destination is still hidden behind a short link (bit.ly…), it is looked up online. Never for one-time or email-provider links.
4. CheckedThe destination's site name is checked against security services, and the link is checked for tricks such as text that shows a different site.
5. ShownSafe links point straight to their destination. Dangerous ones are marked in red and ask before opening.

When you send an email (Gmail), the links you wrote are checked before it goes: typos such as “.con” or “http//”, whether each website exists and works, and whether it's a look-alike, phishing or malware site. If something's wrong, you see which links and why, and choose Go back and fix or Send anyway.

In your Gmail inbox, every sender gets its site's icon, a country flag and a label with its domain, colored by a check of the sender: its address against phishing-sender lists and its domain against the same security services as links. Emails from a flagged sender are moved to Spam for you, once, with a short notice to review them. See Sender icons & Spam.

Everything you see is also available in this window: the Link Inspector shows each step for any link you paste, and Logging holds the application log (every problem and event), the history of checked links and the URL cache. Technical readers will find every detail in the architecture deep dive.

When an email is moved to Spam

  • Only when its sender is flagged. Unverified, unknown, caution and clean senders are never moved.
  • Only from your inbox (including its category tabs), as a list or opened. Never from Spam, Trash, Sent, labels, searches or All mail.
  • Gmail's own Report spam is used, exactly as if you clicked it; if Gmail asks “Report spam and unsubscribe?”, only plain Report spam is chosen (unsubscribing would confirm your address to a phisher). Anything unexpected is cancelled.
  • Never while you're busy: not while you're typing, have a menu or dialog open, or have selected emails yourself (they would be reported too). It waits and tries again.
  • Once per conversation. If you take an email back out of Spam (Not spam, or Undo right away), the extension remembers it and never moves it again. To stop a sender being flagged, add its domain to Always allowed (your organization's or your own phishing-sender entries still win).
  • Never your organization's phishing-training emails (by their simulation domains, or their header rules: an unread training email whose headers can't be checked is left alone), and never emails from your own organization's domain, unless you or your organization listed that exact address (for example a hacked account).
  • Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo and other big providers' domains, and big companies' own domains, are never judged as a whole: there, only exact addresses on a list count.

Lists of phishing senders

Three lists are compared on this device: your organization's (policy managedPhishingSenders), your own (Settings → Senders → Phishing senders), and the community list sefinek/Blacklisted-Emails (MIT license), downloaded with the phishing lists at most once a day. From the community list only exact addresses count, never whole domains, and never addresses at big companies' own domains.

Privacy

Nothing about your emails is sent anywhere. The checks send only: each sender's domain to the threat-check services (as for links), its site name to Google's icon service, and a sending server's IP address to country.is. Emails' headers are read from Gmail itself (your own session), and unread emails are never opened to read them, so nothing can change what Gmail shows as unread. At Offline only none of these requests are made. Details: Your privacy.

Your privacy

The extension has no account, no analytics and no server of its own. Your email's text, subject, sender's address, recipients and attachments are never sent anywhere. (In Gmail, the sender's domain can be checked like a link's site name; see below.)

Never leaves your browser

  • Email content. Only link addresses, link text and image addresses are examined, locally.
  • One-time links (password reset, sign-in, verification, unsubscribe): never sent, cached or logged in full.
  • Email-provider tracking links (Mailchimp, SendGrid, Klaviyo…): they identify you personally, so they are never sent to anyone.
  • Full links to threat-check services: they receive only the site name.

Troubleshooting

Every problem the extension meets is recorded in Logging → Application log with a plain explanation. Common situations:

What you noticeLikely cause and fix
Links say "Not verified"The threat-check services can't be reached, often because a company firewall or web filter blocks DNS-over-HTTPS. Ask IT to allow the services listed under Your privacy, or set your organization's own resolver.
An email shows a banner about company links (c.gle, g.co, aka.ms)Their destination couldn't be looked up, so they're trusted only when Gmail verified the email as sent by that company directly to you. The banner appears when they're in a forwarded part, in a re-sent or mailing-list copy, from another sender, or when the email's verification couldn't be read (see the application log). Outside Gmail the sender can't be verified, so such links are always mentioned.
A link in History says “can't work”Its domain doesn't exist, or exists without any website address (for example test.com), so nobody can reach it. It isn't dangerous, but it won't open.
Send is held up by “Check the links before sending”, but the link is fineThe website may have been briefly unreachable, or be reachable only inside your company network. Choose Send anyway. To stop checking, turn off Check links before sending in Settings.
A short link is amber: "its destination couldn't be looked up"The lookup failed and the reason is in the tooltip. Deploying in organizations

Install the extension for everyone automatically and set (or lock) its settings centrally: with Google Workspace for Chrome, and with Microsoft Intune for Microsoft Edge (or Chrome on Windows).

Before you start. Admin consoles change their menus from time to time; the paths below describe the 2026 consoles and may be worded slightly differently in yours. Always test with a small pilot group first and check the result at chrome://policy or edge://policy.

1. Choose how the extension is distributed

  • Chrome Web Store (recommended for Chrome): publish it as Public, Unlisted, or Private (visible only to your Google Workspace domain). Its extension ID is the 32-letter code in the store address.
  • Microsoft Edge Add-ons (recommended for Edge): publish it there (it can be hidden from search). It gets a different extension ID from the Chrome Web Store version.
  • Chrome Web Store version in Edge: Edge can also force-install the Chrome Web Store version by its Chrome ID and the Chrome update address below.
  • Self-hosting (packed .crx plus an update manifest on your own web server) works only on managed devices; the stores are simpler and update automatically.

Update addresses used in install policies:

Chrome Web Store: https://clients2.google.com/service/update2/crx Edge Add-ons: https://edge.microsoft.com/extensionwebstorebase/v1/crx

The extension's settings are delivered as extension policy: every setting has a policy key (listed in the settings reference and in schema.json). A policy value always wins and appears with 🔒 to the user; anything you don't set stays under the user's control.

2. Google Workspace (Chrome browser and ChromeOS)

  1. In the Google Admin console, go to Devices → Chrome → Apps & extensions → Users & browsers. (To manage Chrome on computers where users don't sign in, enroll the browsers with Chrome Enterprise Core and use the Managed browsers tab instead.)
  2. Select the organizational unit (or group) to deploy to; start with a pilot group.
  3. Click + (Add) → Add from Chrome Web Store and search for the extension, or Add Chrome app or extension by ID and enter its ID (for a self-hosted package also enter its update address).
  4. Set the Installation policy to Force install (or Force install + pin to browser toolbar) so users can't remove it.
  5. In the extension's settings panel, paste your settings into Policy for extensions as JSON. Generate it from Settings → Backup & Google Workspace → Copy as policy JSON and delete the lines you don't want to lock. The format is:
    { "managedEnableThirdPartyResolution": { "Value": true }, "managedDnsCloudflareFamily": { "Value": true }, "managedBlocklist": { "Value": ["tracking.example.com"] } }
  6. Save. Browsers pick up the change at their next policy refresh (usually within a few hours; users can speed it up at chrome://policy → Reload policies).
  7. Check: chrome://extensions shows "Installed by your administrator", chrome://policy lists the extension's policies, and Settings shows 🔒 on the locked options.

3. Microsoft Edge with Microsoft Intune

A. Install the extension automatically.

  1. In the Microsoft Intune admin center, go to Devices → Configuration → Create → New policy; platform Windows 10 and later, profile type Settings catalog.
  2. Click Add settings, search for Microsoft Edge and open the Extensions category.
  3. Enable Control which extensions are installed silently (the ExtensionInstallForcelist policy) and add one entry in the form extension-ID;update-address, for example:
    <Edge Add-ons ID>;https://edge.microsoft.com/extensionwebstorebase/v1/crx <Chrome Web Store ID>;https://clients2.google.com/service/update2/crx
    (Use one of the two. Choose the device-level, not user-level, setting so it applies to everyone on the device.)
  4. Alternatively, configure Extension management settings (ExtensionSettings) with JSON such as {"<ID>": {"installation_mode": "force_installed", "update_url": "<update address>"}}.
  5. Assign the profile to a pilot device or user group, then to everyone.

B. Deliver the extension's settings. Edge reads extension policy from the registry under HKLM\SOFTWARE\Policies\Microsoft\Edge\3rdparty\extensions\<ID>\policy. The Settings catalog doesn't cover third-party extension policy, so use a script:

  1. In this extension, choose Settings → Backup & Google Workspace → Copy registry script. It produces a PowerShell script with your current settings for both Edge and Chrome; replace the extension ID at the top with the ID you deploy.
  2. In Intune, go to Devices → Scripts and remediations → Platform scripts → Add → Windows 10 and later, upload the .ps1 file, and set Run this script using the logged on credentials: No (runs as system) and Run script in 64 bit PowerShell host: Yes.
  3. Assign it to the same groups. To change settings later, upload a new version of the script (or use a Remediation that re-applies it on a schedule).
  4. Check: edge://policy lists the extension's policies; edge://extensions shows the extension as managed; Settings shows 🔒 on locked options.

How values are written (the script does this for you): on/off settings as REG_DWORD 1/0, numbers as REG_DWORD, text as REG_SZ, and the allowed/blocked site lists as a subkey (for example ...\policy\managedBlocklist) with values named 1, 2, 3… each holding one site.

4. Chrome on Windows with Intune (optional)

The same approach works for Google Chrome: in the Settings catalog choose Google Chrome → Extensions → Configure the list of force-installed apps and extensions (ExtensionInstallForcelist, entry <Chrome Web Store ID>;https://clients2.google.com/service/update2/crx). If the Google Chrome settings aren't listed, import Google's Chrome ADMX templates first. The registry script above already writes the settings for Chrome too (HKLM\SOFTWARE\Policies\Google\Chrome\3rdparty\extensions\<ID>\policy).

5. Threat checks with Google Web Risk (Google Workspace)

Google's Web Risk API checks full addresses against Google's lists of phishing and malware pages (the data behind Google Safe Browsing). It needs a Google Cloud project with billing enabled; check Google's current Web Risk pricing first. Note: unlike the built-in checks, which only send site names, Web Risk receives the full link (one-time links are never sent).

  1. In the Google Cloud console, sign in with your Google Workspace account and create or choose a project.
  2. APIs & Services → Library: find Web Risk API and click Enable.
  3. APIs & Services → Credentials → Create credentials → API key. Open the key, choose Restrict key, and under API restrictions allow only the Web Risk API.
  4. In this extension: Settings → Your own services → Custom threat resolver:
    Address: https://webrisk.googleapis.com/v1/uris:search?threatTypes=MALWARE&threatTypes=SOCIAL_ENGINEERING&threatTypes=UNWANTED_SOFTWARE&uri={url} Method: GET Request headers: X-Goog-Api-Key: YOUR_API_KEY Flag field: (leave empty: Google's replies are recognized automatically)
    Click Save service, then Test resolver. Matches show as, for example, "Phishing (Google Web Risk)".
  5. To deploy it to everyone, add it to your policy (the key is hidden from users and never included in backups):
    "managedDohEndpoint": { "Value": "https://webrisk.googleapis.com/v1/uris:search?threatTypes=MALWARE&threatTypes=SOCIAL_ENGINEERING&threatTypes=UNWANTED_SOFTWARE&uri={url}" }, "managedDohMethod": { "Value": "GET" }, "managedDohHeaders": { "Value": "X-Goog-Api-Key: YOUR_API_KEY" }

Google Safe Browsing (v4, threatMatches:find) is also understood, but Google limits it to non-commercial use; organizations should use Web Risk. Short links: Google has no API to expand them; use your own lookup service (Limitations).

6. What can be managed

Every setting, both site lists (allowed and blocked) and both custom services can be set and locked. Anything you don't set stays under the user's control, and Reset my settings to defaults never changes managed settings.

  • Settings and lists: all keys are in the settings reference (generated from the code), in schema.json and in ADMIN-GUIDE.md in the extension package.
  • Your own services: managedUnshortenerEndpoint and managedDohEndpoint with their method, body and header keys. Headers (API keys) set by policy are masked from users and never included in backups.
  • Host access for your own services: Chrome can't grant access to a custom service by policy, so the service should send CORS headers, or users approve a one-time prompt.
  • Senders (Gmail): sender icons, country flags, sender checks, moving flagged emails to Spam and the notice can each be set and locked (managedEnableSenderIdentity, managedEnableSenderCountry, managedEnableSenderDomainCheck, managedEnableSenderAddressCheck, managedEnableAutoSpam, managedEnableSpamNotice), and managedPhishingSenders lists your organization's known phishing senders (addresses or domains). Training emails matched by managedPhishSimulationDomains or managedPhishSimulationRules are never moved.
  • Webmail apps: each supported webmail can be switched on or off (managedEnableOutlookSupport, managedEnableYahooSupport and so on).
  • Changes to policy apply without reinstalling, from the next message opened.

7. Administrator-only policies

These exist only in the policy; there's no setting for them here, and they aren't in backups or in Copy as policy JSON. Full details and examples are in ADMIN-GUIDE.md.

  • managedHidePopupSettingsButton and managedHideRecentActivity remove the Open Settings button and the Recent activity list from the toolbar popup. Settings stays reachable by typing chrome-extension://nnbeoijlicodfplamcelmdlkjggddfla/options.html in the address bar.
  • managedPhishSimulationDomains (every webmail) lists your training vendor's link domains. An email whose links all go to those domains (after unwrapping SafeLinks, Proofpoint and similar) is left completely unchecked; one link anywhere else and it's checked as usual, so real phishing can't hide behind a training link. Shared platforms (such as docs.google.com, sharepoint.com, bit.ly or cloud hosting) are refused, with a warning in the log.
  • managedPhishSimulationRules (Gmail) lists your phishing-simulation (security training) emails, for example from KnowBe4 or Wizer, by the header the vendor adds, a secret value in it, and/or the vendor's sending addresses. In Gmail, a matching email is left completely unchecked: no link is looked up (which the vendor could record as a click), no tracking pixel is blocked and no warning is shown. Use a secret value or the sending addresses: a public header name alone could be copied by real phishing.
"managedPhishSimulationDomains": { "Value": ["ssuport.com", "the-verification.com"] }, "managedPhishSimulationRules": { "Value": [ { "name": "KnowBe4", "header": "X-KB4TOKEN", "value": "YOUR-KSAT-TOKEN" }, { "name": "Wizer", "header": "X-TestPhish", "ips": ["<Wizer's sending addresses>"] } ] }

Best practice: use both identifiers: the vendor's link domains (every webmail) and a header rule with a secret value (Gmail). List only domains the vendor owns, and keep the list current. ADMIN-GUIDE.md has a best-practice checklist and step-by-step setups for KnowBe4 and Wizer, with links to their documentation.

8. Tips

  • Lock only what matters. Settings you leave out of the policy keep their defaults and remain adjustable by users.
  • API keys for your own resolver or unshortener can be delivered by policy (managedDohHeaders, managedUnshortenerHeaders); users see them masked and they are never included in backups.
  • No third-party requests at all: set managedEnableThirdPartyResolution, managedEnableDnsThreatCheck, managedEnableRemoteListSync and managedEnablePhishingFeeds to false; nothing then leaves the browser.
  • Network filters: allow the services listed under every network request, or deliver your own resolver and unshortener by policy.
  • Troubleshooting: if a setting isn't applied, compare its name with the settings reference (names are case-sensitive) and look at chrome://policy / edge://policy, which marks invalid values.

Architecture deep dive

How the extension is built and what it does with every link, in full. Tables marked LIVE are generated from the running code, so they always match the version installed.

Features

Everything the extension does, by area. The sections after this one explain how each part works.

Links

  • Offline decoding of 50+ link wrappers on your device, up to 6 layers deep: email security gateways (Microsoft Safe Links, Proofpoint, Mimecast (domain only), Barracuda, Sophos, Trend Micro, Symantec, Cisco, Check Point Avanan, Libraesva, Forcepoint), search and social redirects (Google, Bing, Yahoo, DuckDuckGo, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Slack, YouTube, Google Meet…), ad and affiliate trackers, and links that carry their destination as encoded data.
  • Short links (bit.ly and 1,700+ other shorteners) looked up with your organization's own service if configured, otherwise unshorten.me; skipped for one-time links and at the Private and Offline levels.
  • One-time link protection: password-reset, sign-in and unsubscribe links are never sent anywhere, cached or logged in full, so they can't be used up.
  • Email click trackers (SendGrid, Brevo, Klaviyo, Mailchimp, HubSpot…) recognized and never sent anywhere automatically; 🔍 reveals the destination on request.
  • Threat checks of every destination with free filtering resolvers: Quad9, Cloudflare Security and Family, Control D, optionally DNS4EU, with AdGuard as backup, or your own service.
  • Phishing lists (OpenPhish community feed, Phishing Army) downloaded once a day and matched on your device.
  • Look-alike domains that imitate well-known brands: look-alike letters, one-letter typos, "brand.com.something.xyz" and brand names next to words like "login".
  • Misleading links in two levels: Caution (amber) for text that shows a different site, raw IP addresses and unusual characters; Danger (red) for look-alike brand domains, text showing a brand's address while going elsewhere, and hidden user names (user@site). Companies' own domains count as one (Gmail and Google, Microsoft and aka.ms…), and file names such as "meson.build" or "README.md" in link text are recognized as files, not websites.
  • Cloud-storage phishing pages (Google Cloud Storage, Firebase, S3, Azure, R2, IPFS) flagged, with hidden "#" data decoded; 🔍 reads the page's code (without running it) to find where it redirects.
  • Checking links before sending (Gmail): when you press Send (or Ctrl/⌘+Enter), the links you wrote are checked for typos in their ending or beginning, websites that don't exist or don't answer, text showing another site, and look-alike, phishing or malware sites. Problems are listed with Go back and fix or Send anyway; emails without links send at once.
  • Sender checks (Gmail): company-only links (c.gle…) whose destination is unknown are trusted only in email Gmail verified as sent by that company directly to you; sender names impersonating brands, brand domains failing verification, look-alike sender domains and scam text in genuine company notifications are flagged. Gmail's verdict is read from the message itself, only when needed.
  • Sign-in links that redirect elsewhere (real Microsoft or Google sign-in pages used as redirectors) are marked Caution.
  • Code links (javascript:, data:, vbscript:, blob:, file:) disabled.
  • Your own lists: sites you always trust (allowed) or always block, with the destination of blocked links hidden until you choose to reveal it.
  • Tracking parameters (utm_… and similar) removed from destinations.

QR codes and attachments

  • QR codes in email images read on your device (Chrome's built-in reader where available, otherwise the bundled decoder) and checked like links; "DO NOT SCAN" when dangerous.
  • QR codes drawn without an image (HTML tables or block characters) found and decoded.
  • Attachments (Gmail, up to 10 MB): QR codes and links in images and PDFs (first 10 pages); scripts, redirects and fake login forms in SVG and HTML files; links in calendar invites; password-protected archives; file names disguised with hidden characters.

Tracking pixels

  • Known open-tracking addresses blocked at any size; otherwise only tiny or hidden images, so real pictures stay.
  • Choice between removing pixels and just neutralizing them.

Where it works

  • Gmail (full support), plus Outlook on the web, Proton Mail, Yahoo Mail, AOL Mail, Zoho Mail, iCloud Mail, Fastmail and GMX (experimental, each can be switched off).
  • Chrome and Microsoft Edge.

Privacy and control

  • Three privacy levels: Standard, Private (only site names are checked) and Offline only (nothing leaves the browser).
  • Results cached for a set time; history, cache and logs deleted automatically after a period you choose.
  • Backup and restore of settings (API keys are never included).
  • Application log of every problem and event, with export for support.

For organizations

  • Every setting, list and custom service can be set and locked by policy (Google Workspace, Microsoft Intune, Group Policy); managed settings show 🔒 and are never changed by a reset.
  • One-click export as Google Workspace policy JSON or as a Windows registry script for Intune.
  • Your own short-link and threat-check services, with API keys hidden from users.

Using it

  • Toolbar icon in color with a count while an email is being checked, grey otherwise; the popup shows the open email's links (scanned, safe, unsafe, not verified, unscanned) and pixels.
  • Light and dark appearance (follows the system by default).
  • Link Inspector: paste any link to see every decoding step and the check result.
  • Help that explains every warning, and this deep dive with tables generated from the running code.

Accessibility

  • Designed to meet WCAG 2.1 level AA: text contrast of at least 4.5:1 (3:1 for controls) in both light and dark appearance, checked automatically on every page.
  • Fully usable with the keyboard: visible focus outline, arrow keys between tabs, Escape closes dialogs.
  • Screen-reader names for every switch, field and button; tabs, menus and dialogs announce what they are.
  • Follows the system's "reduce motion" setting.

Efficiency and reliability

  • Links to the same site share one set of checks; results and settings are cached in memory; log and history writes are batched (an email with 100 links costs about 100 storage reads and 2 writes).
  • QR and PDF scanning runs in a hidden page only when needed, at most two at a time, and closes when idle; only likely QR images are scanned.
  • Third-party lists at most once a day, with safeguards against broken or tampered downloads and a bundled fallback list.

Design principles

  • Local first. Everything that can be worked out on the device is: 50+ wrapper formats are decoded offline before any network request is considered.
  • The destination is never contacted. The extension never requests a link's own server. Short links are expanded by a lookup service; threat checks send only a hostname to DNS resolvers.
  • Fail safe, never fail "safe". Anything that can't be checked is "not verified", never "safe". Unreadable replies count as no answer. A decoder that doesn't recognize a format leaves the link unchanged.
  • One-time links are protected. One-time links are never sent anywhere, cached or logged in full, and only gateway decoders that return the complete original URL may touch them.
  • Untrusted input stays untrusted. Email content, community lists, backup files and custom-service replies are validated, size-limited and parsed defensively.
  • No third-party code. No libraries, no build step, no remote code, no analytics. Every line that runs is in the package.
  • Efficient by design. Nothing runs in the background on a timer except the daily list update and cleanup. Settings are read once and kept in memory until they change; each site is checked once even when many links point to it; log, history and cache writes for a whole email are combined into one storage write; the toolbar badge updates once per burst; and state for messages Gmail has closed is released.

Components

The link pipeline, step by step

From the moment a message opens to the moment a link is marked. Function names refer to the source files.

  1. Detect content.js · MutationObserver A single observer on document.body watches for Gmail inserting a message body (.ii.gt). Bursts of changes are debounced (350 ms), then the scan runs once.
  2. Prepare unshortenLinks Settings are fetched from the service worker (getSettings). Hidden links are disabled, tracking pixels handled, then every <a> with an http(s) host other than mail.google.com is sent once per unique address (resolveUrl), together with its visible text, image alt text and aria-label (used for one-time detection).
  3. Blocklist resolveLink · blocklistHit If the link's own host is blocklisted, the answer is immediate: flagged, destination hidden, nothing decoded, checked or sent. A later reveal re-enters the pipeline with reveal: true (as does revealing an email tracking link).
  4. Allowlist The link is decoded offline; if both it and its destination are allowlisted, it is left untouched.
  5. Cache Ordinary links are answered from the local cache when possible; the blocklist is re-applied to cached destinations.
  6. Decode offline decoders.js · unwrapNestedUrl Up to six layers are peeled, trying gateway decoders first, then the redirect table, JSON payloads, base64, query parameters and path-embedded URLs. For one-time links only gateway decoders run.
  7. One-time check isSingleUseToken The outer link, the decoded link (twice URL-decoded) and the link text are matched against the patterns listed below. A one-time link skips straight to the threat check with its full address withheld from every service.
  8. Eligibility networkEligibility If the destination is still hidden, the rules below decide whether it may be looked up online.
  9. Look up lookupShortLink Custom unshortener first (if set), then unshorten.me (if allowed). The result is re-checked for one-time patterns.
  10. Clean cleanTrackingParams utm_*, fbclid, gclid, mc_cid, mc_eid and _bta_tid are removed, only when present (the URL is otherwise left byte-for-byte).
  11. Assess assessSafety · checkDomainSafety Blocklist first, then the destination and (if different) the link's own host are checked in parallel; the worse result wins, so the verdict is valid whichever address the click actually reaches.
  12. Record Conclusive results are cached; inconclusive, blocklisted and revealed ones never are. History and the activity feed are updated; problems go to the error log.
  13. Show content.js · detectLinkProblem · applyLinkStyles Misleading-link checks run against the visible text, then the link is rewritten or marked. The sender's original styling and markup are saved and restored exactly when highlights are switched off.

Offline decoders LIVE

Each decoder recognizes its format by the parsed hostname (exact host or real subdomain) plus the path, never by text elsewhere in the URL, so https://evil.example/?x=urldefense.com is not treated as Proofpoint. Decoded results must be valid http(s) URLs. Order matters: the first decoder that matches wins, then the loop repeats on the result.

One-time link detection LIVE

A link is treated as one-time if any of these regular expressions match. The patterns deliberately err on the side of caution: a false positive only means a link isn't looked up.

Short-link lookup rules

Evaluated in order by networkEligibility; the first rule that applies decides.

  1. "Look up short links online" off → never.
  2. Email-provider tracker → only when the user reveals it with its 🔍 (and "Offer to reveal email tracking links" is on). Never automatically: it is unique to the recipient, following it registers a click, and it may wrap an unsubscribe or sign-in link.
  3. Blocklisted host → only when the user reveals it.
  4. Built-in public shortener → yes.
  5. Domain only on a synced community list → only if the URL has a short-link shape (no query or fragment, one or two path segments of up to 32 characters).
  6. Unknown domain that looks like a short link (two-label domain with a two-letter country ending, one 4–12 character code with digits or mixed case) → yes.
  7. Anything else → no request.

The lookup chain is the custom unshortener (if configured), then unshorten.me unless its fallback is switched off. Custom replies are read from the configured field or common fields (resolved_url, long_url, final_url, destination…, one level deep) or a plain-text URL; a reply that merely echoes the request is ignored.

Threat-check engine LIVE

For each hostname, a non-filtering reference lookup and all enabled services run in parallel. Filtering resolvers signal a block by answering with a sinkhole address (0.0.0.0, 127.0.0.1); Quad9 answers NXDOMAIN, which is told apart from a genuinely missing domain by the reference lookup.

  • unsafe if any security or content service blocks it; unknown ("not verified") if no security service answered or the domain doesn't exist; otherwise safe.
  • The AdGuard backup is asked only when no primary security service answered; its blocks are ignored for known email-tracker and shortener hosts, which it blocks by design.
  • Conclusive verdicts (safe from the primary services, or unsafe) are kept in memory for (at most 1,000 sites, cleared whenever settings change); unknown is never kept. Links to the same site checked at the same moment share one round of requests. A custom resolver that receives full URLs is cached per URL rather than per host.
  • Custom-resolver replies are understood for DNS-over-HTTPS JSON, Safe Browsing (matches, {} = clean), URLhaus (query_status), simple malicious/blocked fields, or a configured flag field. Anything else (e.g. an invalid-key error) counts as no answer.
  • Timeouts: 6 s per DNS request, 10 s per link lookup, 15 s per list download. Requests are made without cookies.

Misleading-link and pixel heuristics

  • Misleading links (detectLinkProblem): user:password@ hosts; IPv4 or IPv6 literal hosts; any xn-- (punycode) label; and link text that names a domain whose registrable domain differs from the destination's. Registrable domains are approximated without the full public-suffix list (two-letter country endings with common second levels such as co.uk). Text that ends in a file extension (report.pdf), equals the link's own domain, or points through an unresolved built-in tracker is not compared.
  • Hidden links: inline font-size that parses to 0, or opacity ≤ 0.01.
  • Tracking pixels: the original image URL is recovered from Gmail's proxy (after #). An image is a pixel if its host is a built-in tracker or blocklisted, its host or path follows tracker conventions, or its size marks it as a beacon: any dimension over 20 px is never a pixel; ≤ 3×8, area ≤ 24 px², a single dimension ≤ 3 px, inline-hidden or inside a collapsed zero-height box is. Images whose size is unknown are re-checked when they finish loading. Allowlisted hosts are skipped.
